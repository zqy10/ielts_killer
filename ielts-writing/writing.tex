The sample demonstrates Band 9 economy: it never lists all data mechanically but selects the most telling contrasts — Manhattan's proportional collapse versus the outer boroughs' rise. Cohesion is managed through 'In contrast', 'This trend continued', and temporal anchoring ('By 1900', 'By the year 2000'). Lexical precision is apparent in 'the overwhelming majority', 'far exceeding', and 'shifted away from ... to'. One weakness is word repetition ('population' 6×, 'million' 5×, 'total' 4×), which a Band 8.5 response would mitigate through synonyms ('headcount', 'residents', 'citywide figure').

The three tables illustrate how the population of New York City changed between 1800 and 2000, showing the citywide total alongside the figures for Manhattan and the four outer boroughs (Brooklyn, Bronx, Queens, and Staten Island) as both headcounts and proportions.

\par

Overall, the city's population expanded enormously over the two centuries, while its distribution \hi{underwent a striking reversal}: Manhattan's dominant share contracted sharply as the outer boroughs \hi{absorbed the vast majority of growth}.

\par

In 1800, the city totalled just 79,216 residents, three-quarters of whom (76\%) lived in Manhattan. \hi{Rapid urbanisation} drove the citywide figure to approximately 3.4 million by 1900 — \hi{a roughly 43-fold increase} — yet Manhattan's share had already fallen to 54\% (1.85 million). The trend deepened over the following century: by 2000, Manhattan's proportion had \hi{plummeted to just} 19\% (1.54 million), and \hi{its absolute headcount had in fact declined} from the 1900 peak.

\par

The outer boroughs \hi{mirrored this pattern in reverse}. Their combined population of 18,701 in 1800 (24\% of the total) surged to 1.59 million by 1900, pushing their share to 46\%. Growth accelerated further over the 20th century: by 2000, the four districts collectively housed around 6.47 million people — 81\% of all New Yorkers — vastly outpacing Manhattan and marking \hi{a profound redistribution of the city's demographic weight}.

\subsection{教育 (Education)}

\writingprompt{Task 2}{discussion}{教育 Education}{Some people believe that traditional schools are the best environment for children to learn and develop. Others argue that educating children at home provides a better, more tailored preparation for their future. Discuss both these views and give your own opinion.}

\begin{samplebox}
\textbf{参考范文} \textcolor{gray}{\small (来源：\href{https://englishlanguagestudies.com/ielts-writing-task-2-discuss-both-views-guide/}{查看原文})}\par\vspace{3pt}
The debate regarding the most effective environment for childhood education is \hi{highly contentious}. While a significant portion of society argues that traditional educational institutions are unparalleled for holistic development, others maintain that individualised home education offers superior preparation for the future. Although traditional schools foster vital interpersonal skills, I believe that educating children at home provides a more rigorous and tailored academic foundation.

\par

On the one hand, proponents of traditional schooling emphasise the importance of social development. In a conventional classroom, children are consistently exposed to a diverse array of peers and authority figures. This environment naturally \hi{cultivates essential soft skills} such as teamwork, conflict resolution, and empathy. For instance, when students are required to collaborate on group projects or participate in team sports, they learn how to \hi{navigate social dynamics} and communicate effectively. Many argue that these daily social interactions are incredibly difficult to replicate in a home environment and are crucial for thriving in a modern, collaborative workplace.

\par

On the other hand, advocates for home education argue that one-on-one instruction allows for a highly customized learning experience. In a crowded classroom, teachers are forced to teach at a generalized pace, which can leave struggling students behind or cause gifted students to lose interest. Conversely, home education allows the curriculum to be \hi{uniquely tailored to the child's specific strengths and weaknesses}. For example, preparing a younger student at home for rigorous standardized exams allows them to accelerate through subjects they grasp easily while spending extra time mastering complex topics. I personally agree with this view, as this targeted, distraction-free preparation ultimately \hi{maximizes a student's academic potential} and equips them with strong independent study habits.

\par

In conclusion, both educational environments possess distinct advantages. While traditional schools are excellent for nurturing social skills and peer collaboration, I maintain that educating a child at home yields better academic outcomes. The ability to customize the pace and focus of the curriculum ensures that students are perfectly prepared for the specific intellectual challenges of their future.
\end{samplebox}

\begin{analysisbox}
\textbf{亮点分析}\par\vspace{2pt}
The essay opens with a clear thesis signalling the writer's position while acknowledging the opposing view — a hallmark of Band 9 task achievement. Each body paragraph dedicates a full 100-word unit to one side, using a topic sentence, supporting detail, and a concrete example. 'On the one hand / On the other hand' creates clean signposting. The conclusion mirrors the introduction without merely repeating it, reaffirming the opinion with fresh phrasing. Lexical choices ('contentious', 'cultivates', 'navigate social dynamics', 'uniquely tailored') are precise and varied. The shift from third-person exposition to first-person alignment ('I personally agree') in body paragraph 2 is rhetorically effective, grounding the argument in the writer's voice.
\vspace{3pt}\textbf{高分表达}\par
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \hi{highly contentious} （极具争议性的） \textcolor{gray}{——强于 controversial，Academic Word List 高频词}
\item \hi{cultivates essential soft skills} （培养基本软技能） \textcolor{gray}{——搭配 cultivate + noun phrase；用于教育/职场语境}
\item \hi{navigate social dynamics} （驾驭社交关系/人际互动） \textcolor{gray}{——高级替换 deal with social situations}
\item \hi{uniquely tailored to the child's specific strengths and weaknesses} （完全针对孩子的个人优缺点定制） \textcolor{gray}{——因材施教的地道表达}
\item \hi{maximizes a student's academic potential} （最大化学生的学业潜能） \textcolor{gray}{——maximize potential 是学术写作高频搭配}
\item \hi{a generalized pace} （统一的教学进度） \textcolor{gray}{——指班级整体进度，与 tailored pace 形成对比}
\item \hi{distraction-free preparation} （无干扰的备考环境） \textcolor{gray}{——复合修饰语，精练而有力}

\essaymeta{8.5+}{304}

The question of whether children develop more fully in conventional schools or through home-based instruction \hi{continues to generate considerable controversy among educators and parents alike}. Traditionalists argue that formal schooling delivers both academic rigour and vital social experience, whereas advocates of home education maintain that individualised tuition better addresses each child's unique needs. Although both arguments carry genuine weight, I contend that traditional schooling \hi{lays a more robust foundation} for children's long-term development.

\par

Those who champion conventional schooling point to its \hi{irreplaceable social dimension}. Daily interaction with diverse peers requires children to negotiate differences, collaborate on tasks, and communicate under real-world pressure — experiences that \hi{cultivate the interpersonal competencies} that virtually every employer now regards as indispensable. Furthermore, a certified teaching staff and structured curriculum ensure broad subject coverage and \hi{objectively benchmarked academic progression}, giving students a coherent learning trajectory that extends reliably from early childhood through to the end of secondary education.

\par

Proponents of home education, by contrast, emphasise the freedom to design a curriculum around the individual child. In a standard classroom, instruction must be pitched at an average level, which risks \hi{leaving gifted learners under-stimulated and struggling students overwhelmed}. Home tuition eliminates this constraint: a curious child can explore areas of genuine interest in depth, while one who finds a subject difficult \hi{receives sustained, focused attention without the added pressure of peer comparison}. Advocates often cite evidence that home-educated pupils outperform their conventionally schooled peers in university admissions tests.

\par

In conclusion, while home education clearly \hi{offers a degree of personalisation that traditional classrooms cannot match}, I maintain that the social architecture of formal schooling remains essential to the formation of well-rounded individuals. Academic shortfalls can be addressed through tutoring, but the collaborative intelligence and resilience \hi{forged through communal learning} are qualities far more difficult to develop in isolation, and they underpin sustained success throughout adult life.

\subsection{科技 (Technology)}

\writingprompt{Task 2}{opinion}{科技 Technology}{Some people believe that technology has made man more social. To what extent do you agree or disagree with this opinion?}

\begin{samplebox}
\textbf{参考范文} \textcolor{gray}{\small (来源：\href{https://www.ieltspodcast.com/writing-task-2/band-9-sample-essays/technology-social/}{查看原文})}\par\vspace{3pt}
Experts throughout both the developing and developed world have debated whether \hi{the advent of sophisticated modern technology} such as mobile phones, laptops and iPads have helped to enhance and improve people's social lives or whether the opposite has become the case.

\par

Personally, I strongly advocate the former view. This essay will discuss both sides using examples from the UK government and Oxford University to demonstrate points and prove arguments.

\par

On the one hand there is ample, powerful, almost daily evidence that such technology can be detrimental especially to the younger generation who are more easily affected by its addictive nature and which can result in people feeling more isolated from society. The central reason behind this is twofold. Firstly, the invention of online social media sites and apps, such as Twitter and Facebook, have \hi{reduced crucial face-to-face interactions dramatically}. Through use of these appealing mediums, people feel in touch and connected yet lack key social skills and the ability to communicate. Secondly, dependence on such devices is built up frighteningly easily, which may have a damaging effect on mental health and \hi{encourage a sedentary lifestyle}.

\par

On the other hand, although there are significant downsides to technological developments, its multifold advantages cannot be denied. This is largely because the popularity of technology such as cellphones allows people to connect freely and easily \hi{with no geographical barriers}. People are able to share any type of news, information, photos and opinions with their loved ones whenever and wherever they want, therefore \hi{keeping a feeling of proximity and closeness}.

\par

From the arguments and examples given I firmly believe that overall communication and man's sociability has been advanced enormously due to the huge technological progress of the past twenty years and, despite some potentially serious health implications which governments should not fail to address, it is predicted that its popularity will continue to flourish in the future.
\end{samplebox}

\begin{analysisbox}
\textbf{亮点分析}\par\vspace{2pt}
This model answer scores well on Task Response and Coherence: it states a clear position early ("I strongly advocate the former view"), then devotes one body paragraph to each side with the discourse markers "On the one hand / On the other hand", and uses concrete (if invented) studies as support — a useful technique for adding specificity under exam conditions.

\par

Its real strength is Lexical Resource: precise, less-common collocations ("the advent of sophisticated modern technology", "detrimental to the younger generation", "encourage a sedentary lifestyle", "no geographical barriers") rather than generic vocabulary. Note, however, that the original contains several slips ("it's" for "its", "huge the huge") — at a genuine Band 8.5+ these would be ironed out, which is what my own version aims to do while reusing the strongest phrasing.
\vspace{3pt}\textbf{高分表达}\par
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \hi{the advent of sophisticated modern technology} （先进现代科技的出现/到来） \textcolor{gray}{——升级 the appearance of new technology}
\item \hi{be detrimental to} （对……有害） \textcolor{gray}{——替代 be bad for}
\item \hi{reduce crucial face-to-face interactions} （减少关键的面对面交流） \textcolor{gray}{——讨论社交/科技题常用}
\item \hi{encourage a sedentary lifestyle} （助长久坐不动的生活方式） \textcolor{gray}{——健康/科技题高分搭配}
\item \hi{with no geographical barriers} （没有地理障碍） \textcolor{gray}{——替代 from anywhere}
\item \hi{keep a feeling of proximity and closeness} （维持亲近感） \textcolor{gray}{——描写远程沟通维系关系}
\item \hi{hollow out the quality of those connections} （掏空/削弱这些联系的质量） \textcolor{gray}{——我的范文用法，强调深度受损}
\end{itemize}
\end{analysisbox}

\essaymeta{8.5+}{268}

It is frequently claimed that modern digital technology has rendered humanity more sociable than ever before. While I accept that such tools have undeniably widened our networks of contact, I would argue that they have simultaneously \hi{hollowed out the quality of those connections}, making the assertion only partially true.

\par

There is certainly compelling evidence that technology has expanded our social reach. Instant-messaging platforms and video-calling applications allow individuals to \hi{sustain relationships across vast geographical distances} that would once have severed them entirely. A student studying overseas, for instance, can now converse face to face with family members on a daily basis, thereby preserving a sense of closeness that previous generations could scarcely have imagined. In this respect, the sheer breadth of human contact has grown exponentially.

\par

Nevertheless, this proliferation of contact should not be mistaken for genuine sociability. For many people, the convenience of communicating through a screen has displaced the richer interactions that occur in person, fostering relationships that are \hi{wide but shallow}. Few people would dispute that access to clean water is a fundamental human right. It does not follow, however, that this right obliges governments to pipe unlimited water into every home at no cost. While I fully accept that no one should be denied water because of poverty, I disagree with the proposal for an entirely free domestic supply, since \hi{a moral entitlement does not automatically translate into a free commodity}.

\par

The first problem is financial. Although rain falls freely, delivering safe water to a tap does not: treating, storing and distributing it are \hi{energy-intensive processes} that require constant investment in reservoirs, purification plants and pipelines. If households pay nothing, these costs are simply \hi{shifted onto general taxation}, crowding out spending on schools and hospitals.
